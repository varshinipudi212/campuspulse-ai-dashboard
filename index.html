Seven-day execution plan

Each day produces a tangible output and a measurable learning objective.
DAY 1 · FOUNDATION

Instrument the funnel

Finalize the workshop value proposition, registration form, channel tags, referral codes and dashboard definitions.

DAY 2 · AMBASSADORS

Prepare campus distribution

Create concise share copy and a unique referral code for each participating ambassador.

DAY 3 · STUDENT COMMUNITIES

Activate club-led distribution

Prepare club-specific copy and separate source tags for relevant student communities.

DAY 4 · EXPERIMENT

Test message clarity

Compare the specific “Build Your First AI Project in 60 Minutes” proposition with generic workshop wording.

DAY 5 · ANALYZE

Compare quality, not just reach

Review unique registrations, duplicates, attribution, visitor-to-registration conversion and referral quality.

DAY 6 · OPTIMIZE

Shift effort where evidence supports it

Prioritize channels only when the observed conversion and cost justify scaling the work.

DAY 7 · DECIDE

Report outcomes and next steps

Compare verified unique registrations with the 500 target, summarize learnings and set the next experiment.

MEASUREMENT FRAMEWORK

Define success before launch

Primary KPI: Verified unique registrations. Diagnostic KPIs: conversion rate, duplicate rate, cost per registration and referral quality.

Experiment design

01 · Referral attribution

Hypothesis: Unique ambassador codes will reveal which campus groups capture the highest-quality registrations.

  • Track unique codes per ambassador
  • Measure registration completion rate
  • Compare repeat participation or duplicates

02 · Message test

Hypothesis: A practical “Build Your First AI Project in 60 Minutes” headline will outperform generic workshop messaging.

  • Split audience by message version
  • Review registrar-to-visitor conversion
  • Keep the stronger offer only if the uplift is real

03 · Channel scoring

Hypothesis: Not all traffic quality is equal; rankings should reflect conversion and cost, not just reach.

  • Score by cost per qualified lead
  • Prioritize sources with both volume and quality
  • Pause poor-fit channels quickly

AI worklog: prompt → output → review → decision

Illustrative worklog examples documenting how AI can support planning. These are proposed examples for this submission and not verified results.

Prompt: “Suggest a 7-day growth experiment for a 1-hour AI workshop, based on a ₹2,000 budget and a 500-registration target.”

AI output: “Use ambassador referral codes, split-test two headlines, and track cost per qualified registration by channel.”

Review: “This is actionable, measurable and tied to clear KPIs. Keep it, but require a dashboard so quality is not confused with reach.”

Decision: “Launch with attribution tags, daily review, and a kill-switch for any channel below the conversion threshold.”
